# Fit check

The team's materials were written for particular content (for example, one program's flyers). People will use this skill for other things. The fit check makes sure you use what applies, adapt what partly applies, and are honest about the rest. Read the scope **from the materials themselves** each time; don't assume it, since the materials change.

## 1. Describe the content

- **Type:** flyer, web page, web/app UI text, letter or notice, form, FAQ, email, social post, legal or policy text…
- **Program and topic:** which program or service (e.g. paid family leave, SNAP, child care licensing), or general.
- **Audience and register:** the public, applicants, providers, employers, staff; how formal; reading level.
- **Stakes:** does it affect rights, eligibility, money, or deadlines?

## 2. Describe what the materials cover

- **Translation prompt:** the content type it says it's for, the programs and audience it names, its sections (criteria, examples, legal reference material).
- **Glossary:** the program(s) its terms come from (look at the entries and their Definition/Context, not just the sheet name); how many entries are relevant to this content.
- **Extraction prompt** (flyers/PDFs only): it's about reading layout, so it fits any visual document.

## 3. Sort the guidance

| Bucket | What goes in it | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Applies fully** | Rules that don't depend on content type or program | Formal "usted"; plain language; accuracy first; Spanish punctuation and capitalization; dates, numbers, currency; acronyms and official names; URLs and phone numbers unchanged |
| **Applies with adjustment** | Rules written for the materials' content type or audience, adapted here | Flyer tone and brevity on a web page; examples written for workers applied to providers; space-fitting advice for a layout that doesn't constrain space |
| **Doesn't apply** | Guidance about things this content doesn't involve | Program-specific glossary terms that don't appear; statute references (not available in this skill) |
| **Gaps** | What the content needs that the materials don't provide | Program or legal terms with no glossary entry; a content type the prompt doesn't address (UI microcopy, legal notices); audience differences |

## 4. Decide

- **Good fit** (same type and program): say so in a line and continue.
- **Partial fit** (different type or program, ordinary stakes): list the adjustments and gaps, then continue. Gap terms are flagged in the notes and proposed as glossary entries; never guess them silently.
- **Poor fit and high stakes** (e.g. a legal notice or eligibility decision when the materials are for flyers about another program): explain, and ask whether to continue. Suggest getting a professional translator involved.

## 5. Report it

Put this before the translation, in 5–10 lines:

> **How the team's materials fit this content**
> - **Content:** web page for child care providers about licensing renewals (public-facing, moderate stakes).
> - **Materials:** the prompt is written for paid family leave flyers for workers; the glossary (108 terms, updated 2026-06-24) covers paid family leave.
> - **Applies fully:** formal "usted", plain language, punctuation, dates and numbers, acronyms.
> - **Adjusting:** flyer tone → web page (headings and links kept as they are; no space limits).
> - **Doesn't apply:** paid leave terms and statute references; none appear in this text.
> - **Gaps:** "licensing specialist", "provisional license", "EXCELS rating" have no glossary entry; I've translated them carefully and flagged them for review.

## Conflicts between materials

If two materials disagree (e.g. date formats, how to mark acronyms), follow the **translation prompt** for wording, and name the conflict in the fit note so the content team can fix it. If the glossary disagrees with the prompt, follow the **glossary** for that term (the prompt says the glossary's approved terms take precedence) and note it.
